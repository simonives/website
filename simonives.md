## Approach

Simon's method is a disposition that emerged from the work itself. Three practices govern how he approaches a problem.

### The Socratic practice

Every problem arrives pre-described. Someone has decided what it is before anyone asks whether that description is true. Simon's first move is to refuse the given framing and find the actual problem underneath it. The stated problem is a claim, not a fact. Most failed transformations solved a problem no one had.

### The utilitarian practice

The measure of a system is the aggregate value it returns to the people inside it. Simon holds to the utilitarianism of Bentham and Singer: a system is worth the good it returns, summed across everyone it touches. Friction is waste, and waste carries moral weight when it is paid in the hours of a global workforce. A system that saves the enterprise money and costs its people their attention has moved the cost somewhere harder to see.

### The polyrhythm of transformation

A complex enterprise never changes at one tempo. Technology deploys in quarters. Behaviour shifts over years. Capital releases on its own cycle, indifferent to both. Governing change means holding these rhythms at once and refusing to force them into unison. Force them together and something breaks: the rollout that arrives before anyone is ready, the funding that lands after the moment has passed. This practice is the least visible from outside the work and the most decisive for whether a programme survives contact with a real organisation.

## Track record

### AI governance

Simon chairs BHP's HR AI Governance Committee, established in April 2026. The committee holds veto authority over every use of generative AI in the HR function. It meets monthly and reports to the HR leadership team each quarter. Its work is deciding what may proceed, what may not, and what is not yet understood well enough to allow.

He built the governance tooling himself: the BOS MCP Server, twenty-nine tools across a three-layer architecture, supported by one hundred and fifty-seven tests. He built it because governing AI from a slide deck is not governing it. The tool is working proof that enterprise AI governance can be engineered.

### Architecture governance

Simon sets the strategy for BHP's HR technology portfolio and governs the capital behind it. The estate is an enterprise-scale capital portfolio spanning SAP SuccessFactors, ServiceNow, Workday, UKG, and the data platforms beneath them.

### Platform delivery

Simon has delivered four ServiceNow programmes end to end at BHP, including Employee Relations, Governance Risk and Compliance with its Obligations Register, and GBS Hire-to-Retire. Before BHP he led Glencore's global HXM transformation across SAP SuccessFactors, UKG Dimensions, ServiceNow, and Qualtrics, on five continents. He delivered SuccessFactors across Northern and Western Queensland for the health service.

### People data intelligence

Simon leads a capital programme building the analytical foundation for workforce decisions. It joins Snowflake, SAP Business Data Cloud, and SuccessFactors into one estate, the substrate on which predictive and AI-assisted people analytics can safely stand.

### Contribution

Simon advises SAP's global HR technology leadership and ServiceNow's global HR technology leadership. He serves on the AHRI (Australian HR Institute) Queensland State Council and mentors HR practitioners, including QUT MBA and Masters students, and he sits on the Australian Institute of Business MBA advisory committee. He chaired the Casa Grande Benefit Fund, Glencore's charitable trust for the communities in which it operated.

### Career trajectory

Simon's career is a sustained arc of increasing scope. He leads HR technology strategy at BHP now. Immediately before BHP he worked at Townsville City Council, where he delivered a ServiceNow programme. Before that he was Global Head of HXM Transformation at Glencore, where he built the case for a worldwide technology transformation and delivered its first phase. Earlier he led people and transformation in remote Queensland health at the North West Hospital and Health Service, delivering SuccessFactors across the region. His path into the field ran through employment services in Mount Isa and a formative role on Virgin Australia's UNIFY programme, where he found the capacity to hold an entire enterprise system in view at once.
